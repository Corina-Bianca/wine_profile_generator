# Sparkling wines

Wine Label-to-Profile Skill (/wine-page) by Corina-Bianca Gonteanu, CC BY-NC 4.0.

Read instead of the still-wine rules for ageing potential and storage. For aroma and flavour the sparkling wheel below replaces the still wheel; the building rules in aroma-wheel.md still apply.

## Ageing potential

Bands: under 2 / 2–5 / over 5–10 / over 10 years. The band is shown only when a source states it.

- A range uses its upper figure N (for example, "3 to 4 years" gives N = 4).
- Continuous threshold mapping on the upper figure N (in years):
  - N < 2 years: under 2 years
  - 2 ≤ N ≤ 5 years: 2–5 years
  - 5 < N ≤ 10 years: over 5–10 years
  - N > 10 years: over 10 years
- "Decades" maps to over 10 years.
- WSET Level 3 readiness statement only when exactly one is supported (wording as in ageing-potential.md, checked against the WSET Level 3 SAT, Issue 2, 2022).

Starting point, named in brackets whenever a source gives one:

1. "from disgorgement" when the source gives a disgorgement date or states the potential from disgorgement. Lees ageing is part of production; bottle ageing starts at disgorgement.
2. "from vintage" when the source gives a vintage-based figure and no disgorgement date.
3. Neither stated: show the figure as given, no starting point added.

Example value: "over 5–10 years (from disgorgement): source wording kept".

Non-vintage and tank-method wines usually have no source statement: the row stays blank. Never infer "drink now".

## Storage

Two lines when sources give them: closed bottle (temperature, position, light, directions as given) and after opening (as given). Same priority order as every row.

## Aroma and flavour

Sparkling wines use the sparkling wheel below INSTEAD of the still-wine wheel in aroma-wheel.md. Reason: it adds the autolytic families (Yeasty, Creamy) and splits some families differently, so one wheel per wine type avoids forcing sparkling descriptors into still categories.

All building rules in aroma-wheel.md (source wording, priority order, headings with first and second tier, only categories with entries, "Other", no fault labels, flavour same as aroma) apply unchanged.

## Mouthfeel

Mousse is stated under Mouthfeel on the WSET Level 3 scale (delicate, creamy, aggressive) when the source's wording maps cleanly, else in the source's own words; acidity and body on the WSET scales as for still wines. Bubbles and bead as seen go in Appearance. Sweetness is the dosage category as printed on the label (Brut Nature, Extra Brut, Brut, Extra Dry, Dry, Demi-sec, Doux) or as the source states, shown in the Style row; no figures are inferred.

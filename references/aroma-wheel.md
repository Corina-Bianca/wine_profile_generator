# Aroma and flavour categories (base model)

Wine Label-to-Profile Skill (/wine-page) by Corina-Bianca Gonteanu, CC BY-NC 4.0.
Reference read before filling the Aroma and Flavour rows of still and fortified wines.
Based on the Wine Aroma Wheel by Ann C. Noble (© A.C. Noble 1990, www.winearomawheel.com; the current edition is published by InnoVinum Academy), adapted.
This file holds only category names and our own mapping notes; the wheel's artwork is not reproduced.

## How the rows are built

1. Collect the descriptors the source gives for the row (priority: producer first; if the producer gives none, reputable reviewers of the exact vintage; reviewers may fill what the producer did not give).
2. Keep the source's own wording for each descriptor ("ripe blueberries", not "blueberry").
3. Place each descriptor under the category it belongs to, even if the wheel does not list that exact word
   (membership rule: a descriptor belongs to the second-tier category whose members it resembles).
4. Heading = first tier; add the second tier when one exists and differs from the first: "Fruity · Berry".
   Where the second tier has the same name as the first (Earthy) or none (Floral, Spicy, Nutty, Caramel, Oxidized), show the first tier only.
5. Show only headings that have entries. Fixed display order, below. Within Fruity, follow the second-tier order.
6. A descriptor with no home on the wheel goes under "Other". Never invent a category.
7. Flavour uses the same categories as aroma. Sources often do not separate aroma from flavour. When the source gives one undifferentiated list (or only a nose), repeat the same clusters under Flavour. Palate descriptors and modifiers ("ripe", "concentrated", "fresh") from reputable reviewers of the exact vintage are added under Flavour. When a source does separate nose and palate, keep them separate. Mouthfeel, body, acidity, tannin and finish are not on the wheel: use the WSET Level 3 scales.
8. No fault labels. Chemical, Pungent, Oxidized and Microbiological keep their headings when a source uses those descriptors.
9. Sparkling wines use their own wheel: see sparkling.md. Fortified wines use this wheel plus the mapping in fortified.md.

## Display order and tiers (Noble wheel; 1990 edition and the current InnoVinum Academy edition have the same categories and wording)

| Order | First tier | Second tier |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fruity | Citrus · Berry · Tree fruit · Tropical fruit · Fruit (dried/cooked) · Other |
| 2 | Floral | none |
| 3 | Spicy | none |
| 4 | Herbaceous | Fresh · Dried |
| 5 | Vegetable | Fresh · Canned/cooked |
| 6 | Nutty | none |
| 7 | Caramel | none |
| 8 | Woody | Resinous · Phenolic · Burned |
| 9 | Earthy | Earthy · Moldy (heading shows "Earthy" for the first, "Earthy · Moldy" for the second) |
| 10 | Chemical | Petroleum · Sulfur · Pungent |
| 11 | Oxidized | none |
| 12 | Microbiological | Yeasty · Lactic · Other |
| 13 | Other | not on the wheel |

ADAPTATION (the author's decision): Noble's single family "Herbaceous/Vegetative" is split in two, because herbs (mint, hay) and vegetables (green pepper, olive) are different things.
Herbs: Fresh (cut green grass, mint, eucalyptus) and Dried (hay/straw, tea). Vegetables: Fresh (bell pepper) and Canned/cooked (green bean, asparagus, green olive, black olive, artichoke).
The name "Vegetative" is not used. Everything else is Noble's: 12 first-tier families and 26 second-tier categories here, against 11 and 25 on the wheel.
Credit wording: "Based on the Wine Aroma Wheel by Ann C. Noble, adapted".
Order 1 to 12 is our display order; the wheel itself is circular. "Other" at order 13 is ours, not the wheel's.

Wheel wording kept for second-tier examples (for membership matching only, not displayed unless the source uses them):
Petroleum: tar, plastic, kerosene, diesel. Sulfur: rubber, hydrogen sulfide, natural gas (mercaptan), garlic, skunk, cooked cabbage, burnt match, wet dog/wet wool.
Pungent: sulfur dioxide, ethyl acetate, acetic acid. Yeasty: baker's yeast, leesy. Lactic: sauerkraut, sweaty, yogurt. Microbiological other: horsey, mousey.
Resinous: tobacco, oak, cedar, vanilla. Phenolic: medicinal, bacon. Burned: smokey, burnt toast, coffee. Earthy: dusty, mushroom, musty. Moldy: musty or mouldy (not cork).
Fruity other: artificial fruit, methyl anthranilate (foxy).

## Mapping notes (our own, common descriptors)

| Descriptors | Category |
|---|---|
| lemon, lime, grapefruit, orange peel or zest, mandarin, candied citrus | Fruity · Citrus |
| blueberry, blackberry, raspberry, strawberry, cranberry, blackcurrant or cassis, red or black or wild berries | Fruity · Berry |
| cherry, plum, apricot, peach, apple, pear, quince | Fruity · Tree fruit |
| pineapple, melon, banana, lychee, mango, passion fruit | Fruity · Tropical fruit |
| raisin, prune, fig, jam or jammy, stewed or dried fruit | Fruity · Fruit (dried/cooked) |
| rose, violet, orange blossom, elderflower, acacia, geranium | Floral |
| black pepper, clove, liquorice or anise, cinnamon, nutmeg, spice | Spicy |
| cut grass, mint, eucalyptus, dill, herbs | Herbaceous · Fresh |
| green pepper, bell pepper | Vegetable · Fresh |
| green olive, black olive, asparagus, green beans, artichoke | Vegetable · Canned/cooked |
| hay, straw, tea | Herbaceous · Dried |
| almond, hazelnut, walnut | Nutty |
| honey, butterscotch, butter, toffee, caramel, nougat, chocolate, soy sauce, molasses | Caramel |
| oak, cedar, vanilla, tobacco | Woody · Resinous |
| medicinal, bacon | Woody · Phenolic |
| smoke, toast, charred, coffee | Woody · Burned |
| mushroom, dusty, forest floor, wet earth | Earthy |
| musty, mouldy (not cork) | Earthy · Moldy |
| corked, cork taint, TCA, mouldy cork | not listed (see below) |
| diesel, kerosene, petrol, tar, plastic | Chemical · Petroleum |
| vinegar, nail polish remover, sulfur dioxide | Chemical · Pungent |
| alcohol, hot, menthol | Other (not on the wheel) |
| yeast, lees, bread, bread dough, brioche, biscuit, pastry | Microbiological · Yeasty |
| yogurt, sauerkraut | Microbiological · Lactic |
| horsey, mousey | Microbiological · Other |
| artificial fruit, foxy | Fruity · Other |
| oxidised, sherry-like | Oxidized |
| graphite, flint, wet stone, chalk, mineral, salty, leather, game | Other |

Cork taint: "corked", "cork taint", "TCA" and "mouldy cork" describe a defective bottle, not the wine, and are not mapped to Earthy · Moldy. They are not listed in Aroma or Flavour. (The Noble wheel itself lists "moldy cork" under Moldy; this skill deliberately does not.)
